INTERNATIONAL STUDENT HANDBOOK – SUMMARY OF ACADEMIC RULES, 2026-2027
This handbook summarises, in English, the main rules and procedures that international and exchange students need to know. It does not replace the official texts, which are written in French and prevail in case of doubt. We recommend reading it before arriving at the university and keeping it during the whole year.

1. Registration
Registration takes place at the registrar's office between 14 September and 2 October 2026. The file must include a certified copy of the previous degree, a passport or identity card, four passport photos, academic transcripts and proof of the first payment. Incomplete files are put on hold until the missing documents are provided. Registration closes on 2 October and late files are accepted only with the dean's permission.

2. Registration steps
Registration has five steps: collect the form at the registrar's office, fill it in, hand in the complete file, pay the first instalment of the fees, and wait for the registrar to confirm. Registration is final only after the last step. The student then receives a username for the learning platform and an appointment to collect the student card.

3. Visa and residence
International students need a valid visa or residence permit. On request, the university issues an admission letter to support the visa application. The foreign degree must be recognised by the ministry before the registration is completed. Students must renew their residence permit before it expires and show the new permit to the registrar.

4. Admission letter
The admission letter is requested by email from the registrar's office once the application has been accepted. It states the name of the programme and the dates of the academic year. It is delivered within one week and can be used for the visa application at the embassy. It does not guarantee the visa itself.

5. Orientation week
New students take part in an orientation week during the first week of October. It includes a tour of the campus, a presentation of the programme by the head of the Master, a session on the learning platform and a meeting with the student associations. Attendance is strongly recommended, especially for students who arrive from abroad.

6. Accommodation
The university does not run student residences. The student life office keeps a list of private rooms and shared flats near the campus, and can advise on rents and rental contracts. International students should look for housing before the beginning of the semester, as prices rise in September. The office cannot sign a lease for the student.

7. Tuition and payment
Tuition for the Master's programme is 60,000 FDJ per year. It is paid at the cashier's office or by bank transfer, either in full or in two equal instalments: the first at registration and the second before 15 February 2027. A student who has not paid the second instalment cannot sit the exams of the second semester.

8. Paying by bank transfer
To pay by bank transfer, use the account number printed on the registration form. The transfer reference must show the student's name and registration number. Bring or send the proof of transfer to the cashier within three days; the receipt is issued once the accounting office has checked the payment. Transfers from abroad can take up to two weeks.

9. Financial difficulties
A student who has trouble paying on time can ask the finance office to spread the payments over several dates. The request must include a short explanation and be submitted before the due date of the instalment. The committee answers within ten days and can accept a payment plan of up to four instalments.

10. Refunds
Tuition fees are not refunded once classes have started, except in case of a billing error or when a foreign student is refused a visa. The request is made to the finance office with supporting documents. A refund is paid within thirty days of the acceptance of the request.

11. Receipts and enrolment certificate
Keep the receipt of every payment: it is required to collect the enrolment certificate, which is issued within 48 hours at the registrar's office. The certificate is needed for scholarship, visa and insurance procedures. It is valid only for the current academic year, and a new one must be requested each year.

12. Scholarships
Scholarship applications are submitted in October. They are assessed on academic results and the family's financial situation, and the decision is announced in December. The application file includes a request form, an enrolment certificate, transcripts and proof of family income. Scholarships are limited by the yearly budget.

13. Student card
The student card is issued once the registration is final. It gives access to the library, the computer rooms and the exams. A lost card is replaced for 2,000 FDJ at the registrar's office. Students must always carry the card on campus and show it to security staff when asked.

14. Language of instruction
Courses are taught in French. Some reading material and lab instructions are in English, so students who do not speak French well should take the language support classes offered during the first semester. Exams and reports are written in French unless the teacher agrees to another language.

15. Attendance
Attendance at lectures, tutorials and labs is compulsory. After three unexcused absences in the same subject, the student receives a written warning and may be barred from the exam in that subject. An attendance sheet is signed at every session, and signing for another student is considered as cheating.

16. Sick leave
If you are absent because of illness, give a doctor's note to the registrar's office within three days of returning to class. A note handed in after this deadline is not accepted. Other reasons for absence, such as a bereavement or an official summons, need an official document.

17. Exams and resits
There are two exam sessions: the regular one at the end of each semester and a resit session for subjects that were not passed. Only failed subjects are retaken, and the better of the two marks is kept. The resit session takes place from 21 June to 2 July 2027, and students receive an individual summons.

18. Exam rules
Bring your student card or an identity document to every exam and arrive fifteen minutes early. No candidate is admitted after thirty minutes of delay. Phones, smart watches and earphones must be switched off and left at the front of the room. A device that is switched on during the exam is treated as an attempt to cheat.

19. Grades
Each module grade is made up of 40% coursework and 60% final exam, marked out of 20. A semester is passed with an average of at least 10 out of 20. A student can compensate a weak module with a stronger one in the same semester, provided that each module has at least 7 out of 20.

20. Appeals
A student who disagrees with a mark can ask for a review of the paper by writing to the head of department within five working days after the results are posted. The paper is then reread by a second examiner. The mark may be confirmed, raised or lowered after the review.

21. Learning platform
You log in to the learning platform with the username given at registration. If you forget your password, use the reset link sent to the email address you declared. The link is valid for twenty-four hours. Course materials, assignments and announcements from the teachers are published on the platform.

22. Locked account
After five failed login attempts the account is locked. Go to the IT office (room 12, building B) with your student card to have it unlocked. The office is open from 8:00 to 15:00, Sunday to Thursday. Unlocking is immediate once your identity has been checked.

23. Email and Wi-Fi
Every student has an institutional email address, which is used for official announcements such as timetable changes and exam summons. The campus Wi-Fi works with the same username and password as the platform. Each student can connect two devices at the same time.

24. Internships
The internship office helps students find a placement and keeps a list of partner companies. A training agreement signed by the university, the company and the student is compulsory before the placement starts. The agreement is prepared at least two weeks before the start date. Without it, the internship is not recognised.

25. Health services
A small medical centre on the campus is open from 8:00 to 14:00, Sunday to Thursday. A nurse can give first aid and a doctor is present on Monday and Wednesday mornings. Consultations are free for students who have paid their tuition. In an emergency outside these hours, call the campus security office.

26. Student associations
The university has about fifteen student associations, including a computer club, a sports club and a cultural association. They hold a stand at the orientation week where new students can sign up. Associations can use classrooms in the evening after a request to the student life office.

27. Useful contacts
The registrar's office is open from 8:00 to 14:00, Sunday to Thursday. The finance office is in building A, the IT office in room 12 of building B, and the internship office in building C. The student life office is next to the library and can answer general questions about life on campus.
